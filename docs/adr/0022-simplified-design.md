# The Simplified design is a flag over the same components

The Simplified design is a calmer, native-looking design for the app: SF Pro, fewer parts, a plain desk, a hairline and one soft shadow. It changes layout and chrome, never colours: the theme or pack picked in Appearance gives the same desk, tiles, statuses, zones, accent and notes under either design. It is built beside today's design, Classic, which stays the default until the Simplified one is finished and chosen. We build it as a flag over the same components rather than as a second set of them: one Button, one tile and one session window, each restyled by tokens and, where it must, rendering differently.

## The setting

- **Settings › General › Design › Simplified design** (marked Preview while it is built) turns it on. It is one per user, not per board.
- **Main holds it**, in `design.json` in the app's data folder, beside `appearance.json` and for the same reason: it is read before the first window opens, so no window flashes the other design. A new window's query carries it (`WINDOW_QUERY.design`), and its background is the theme's desk, the same under either design.
- **Every window follows a pick at once**, with no reload: main sends it to every window (`design:changed`), as it does an Appearance pick.

## The flag

- **`data-design='simplified'` on the root** while it is on, set before the first paint (`renderer/src/design.ts`). Under Classic the attribute is absent, not `classic`, so no Classic selector can match it by accident.
- **Restyling is tokens.** Two blocks at the end of `tokens.css`, `:root[data-design='simplified']` and `:root[data-design='simplified'][data-theme='dark']`, set the Simplified values of tokens Classic already defines: its shapes, type, rail width, shadows and the dim behind a sheet, with no dots on the desk and a fainter zone tint (`--zone-fill-mix`, 5% in light and 4% in dark against Classic's 8% and 6%; a zone's edge is Classic's). They add no token of their own and set no colour of the palette, so every look draws each palette token as Classic does in the same theme (`tokens.test.ts`); every colour the light block's shadows write, the dark one writes too. Components keep reading the same role tokens, so most of the work is in these blocks.
- **Rendering differently is `useSimplified()`**, for the components that must show something else (a different control, fewer parts), not a different look. It reads the root's attribute, so it agrees with the styles in every window.
- **Connections keep today's rails.** Rails, their tokens and captions keep Classic's colours, a little finer and with their tokens worded in the system's face, with the same shapes, routes and **Show connections** modes. A token under the flag has no Send button: a waiting hand-off is sent from its Edge rules.
- **Theme packs carry through.** The theme picked in Appearance shows under the Simplified design as under Classic: `data-theme` is the theme's id under either design, so a pack, its accent and a pick in any window apply at once. Over the app's own themes the design is its two blocks above. Over a pack it is a block of its own per pack theme, `:root[data-design='simplified'][data-theme='<id>']`, made with the pack's Classic block in `theme-packs.css` (`shared/theme-pack-tokens.ts`): it sets every token the design's blocks set in the pack's mode, so it outranks both them and the pack's Classic block. It holds the design's shapes, type and shadows only, so every colour is the pack's own, as under Classic. A new window opens on its theme's desk under either design. Every look meets the contrast checks every theme is held to (`tokens.test.ts`).

## Characters

An agent's character keeps Classic's terracotta under the flag, as every colour does: the shape of its body, not a colour of the design's own, tells agents apart. Terracotta sits near Needs you's orange, and the two stay apart because the board alone draws Needs you, as the tile's orange outline and its word; a body never says it.

## The docked pane

Needs you and the Captain open one docked pane, the Classic Captain panel's frame (its width, its drag handle, its place under the islands), each on its own side: Needs you on the list, the Captain on its chat. Under the flag the Needs-you pill is a plain toggle, not the inbox's popover. Its icon and count are neutral, never orange, and the Captain's button beside it is plain text with no avatar. A click on the side that is open closes the pane; a click on the other button switches the side and keeps the pane open. The pane's side lives in `useMasterPanel()`, and ⌘⇧M always opens the Captain's. Open on a row of the list shows that agent and leaves the pane open, as it is docked, not a popover; the pane is the Captain's whatever it showed when the flag is turned off. The Captain's Watching and For you sections are not drawn under the flag; Classic's panel keeps them.

**Since you last looked** sits under the list on the Needs-you side, and is not drawn on the Captain's side or under Classic. It holds only things that need nothing from the user (what needs them is the list above it): **Done**, the agents that said their work finished (`workFinishedAt`) since the last look, from the app's own record and so always true; and **Up next**, the Captain's own status line (`set_status`), shown only if it wrote it after the last look and is live. The app never writes either line itself, and with nothing to report the card is not drawn. "The last look" is when the list was last on screen, kept in the renderer's storage for every window (`useSinceLastLooked`); it is stamped when the list opens, when it goes and when the window unloads, and the card holds the look before that one still while the list stays up. **Mark as seen** moves it to now. A first look ever shows nothing. Done lists only agents still on the board and still finished: one closed after it finished, or prompted again, is gone from it. An agent waiting at its prompt under "every agent waiting" is Done, not an ask; only a question, plan, permission or flag keeps it out. It never answers, approves or nudges anything: a row only opens the agent.

## The Needs-you list

Under the flag the pane's list is in four sections (`GroupedInbox.tsx`, `needsYouSections`), each oldest first, and an empty one isn't drawn:

- **Continues the work**: permission prompts and plans, which unblock an agent's work.
- **Reaching out to you**: questions and flags, and finished turns where Settings lists them.
- **Ready for you to accept**: an agent at its prompt, or ended, with an open pull request of a branch it owns, asking for nothing (`readyToAccept`). A crew member's changes go to its orchestrator.
- **Problems**: an agent that failed, until the user opens it; one a restart stopped, or one at its usage limit, while it stays so (`isProblem`).

An agent is listed once, under what it asks for first: with finished turns listed, a live agent at its prompt with an open pull request is a finished turn, and only once it has ended is it ready to accept. The first two sections are Needs you's requests (ADR-0016), the same as Classic's list. The last two aren't requests, so they count toward no badge and raise no notification. The split by what a row asks of you replaced the mock's groups by zone, and each row carries its zone as a label (its colour and name).

Every kind has one row: a neutral tile with the kind's glyph (its word for screen readers and on hover), the agent's name, which opens it, its zone, its age, a plain line of what it wants and its answers. ↑ and ↓ move between rows and Return opens the row's agent, never answering; keys 1–9 answer a question only, from its row as from inside it. A permission row is `PermissionRow`, the one the Captain's panel draws, reading as the pane's card under the flag: the sentence from the pane's table, what it acts on in mono, and the card's own buttons (`PermissionButtons`, with its 0.4 s guard and Review… for a destructive call, which lets the harness ask and opens the agent), in their compact order for the narrower list: Allow once beside Deny, and Allow for this session after them as a quiet button. It carries one timer line (`permissionWaitLine`), not a seconds countdown beside the buttons, and the pane's card carries the same line, so a request reads the same wherever it is answered.

**Accept changes** squash-merges the pull request at the commit shown, with the clean-up the zone's merges last used, as Merge does, once the same conditions hold: the gate passed on the PR's head in a zone with a gate, and GitHub allows. It runs 10 seconds after the click (`accept-later.ts`, kept outside React so closing the pane neither drops nor fires it early; quitting the app drops it, merging nothing), and **Undo** cancels it before then. When the moment comes it merges only if the agent is still ready to accept and hasn't finished a turn since the click (`stillReadyToAccept`): a reply sent meanwhile, or anything it asks, drops the accept, and a toast says so. That covers the agent's row leaving the list, and its Undo with it, while the accept waits. Undo never reverts a merge that has run. Someone else's pull request has no Accept. A problem row offers what the tile offers: Resume for an agent a restart stopped, Continue on credits for a Claude agent at its usage limit, and Open. What the list shows is picked in Settings, so its footer saying so is technical (`data-technical-detail`).

## The composer

Under the flag the composer's `>` prompt is a **+** menu, its Send is a round arrow button and its hint line is gone (`Composer.tsx` renders the Classic tree unchanged when the flag is off). The menu holds **Attach an image…** (a picker feeding the same path as a paste), **Summarise the conversation so far** and **Change model…**. The two commands are *typed into the field* (`/compact`, and `/model` with a space after it) for the user to send with Enter, never sent by the app, and wait for an empty field so a draft is never swallowed. The empty field says a sentence for the state the agent is in (`composerPlaceholder`): "Reply…" while it waits on the user, then paused ("sending wakes it"), rate-limited ("your message will wait"), queued until it is up, or the reason the field is off. Enter, Shift+Enter and Option+Enter stay as they are.

## Questions and plans in the pane

Under the flag a question or plan the agent waits on shows above its pane's composer (`PaneDecision.tsx`), in the chat's views, using the same answering as the Needs you inbox (`useAskAnswer`, `PlanDecisionBar`). A question is "‹agent› asks", then a numbered row per choice (keys 1–9 while the frame has focus) and, when it takes words, a "Something else…" reply; a plan is "Plan vN", its title and Read the plan ›, then its comments and Reject · Request changes · Approve, Approve trailing and also pressed with ⌘↵. As it shows, the card takes the keyboard (a question's card itself, so Return never answers; a plan's comments field), unless the user is typing in a field such as the composer or a terminal, whose keys stay theirs. **Decide later** (Esc while the card has focus; Esc in the composer stays the draft's) only puts it off: it stays pending, in Needs you and on its tile, a line above the composer brings it back, and a new question or plan version shows again; the window stays open. What is put off stays put off while the app runs, across closing the window or changing its view. The composer stays beneath it, so a draft is never lost. One decision shows at a time (a question, then a plan; putting off the first shows the next); permission prompts stay in the terminal and the inbox. Classic keeps its chat line and the board's card.

## Agent notes

Under the flag a note an agent writes is drawn as Classic draws it (`AgentNoteFace` has one tree for both designs): its zone-coloured dashed card under the **Note · ‹agent›** tab, which selects the agent's tile and brings it into view while the tile is on the board. There is no coloured stripe down its side, and a note whose agent has no known name (in either design) has a tab that reads just **Note**: never a "by …" or an empty name. The card has one look, so Classic's **Agent notes** setting is not drawn under the flag (its section and search hits go with it) and a saved terminal look is not used. Stickies are not touched: their colours are Classic's, as every colour is, and their lift is pinned to Classic's value in the flag's two token blocks (`--sticky-shadow`), so the design's `--shadow-tile` doesn't move it.

## The shelves under Classic

An agent's sidebar is its **shelves**: a row of icon tabs opening on the agent's own page, with Made, Crew and Setup a tab away, collapsing to a rail whose icons open a shelf as a peek. They replace the inspector, the short grouped list this design first had. They are offered to Classic too, as its **Agent sidebar** setting: Settings › Agents › Sidebar, **Shelves** or **Full**. Full, today's details strip, is the default and stays a choice for one release, so Classic is unchanged until Shelves is picked; a saved Simple, the inspector's old choice, reads as Shelves. Under the Simplified design the sidebar is always the shelves and the setting is not drawn (its section and search hits go with it), as the Agent notes setting is not.

- **Kept in the view state** (`agentSidebar`), with the board's other view settings, so every window follows a pick at once and an agent window open as it is picked swaps its sidebar there and then; the choice is read with the design (`useAgentSidebar()`), never on its own.
- **One component.** The same shelves in Classic's tokens: their CSS reads role tokens only, and an agent's tones are the shapes' palette. Their width and handle are the details strip's, and whether they are open or on the rail is one choice for every window, shared by the designs.
- **The rail is their collapsed form.** Pulled below the least, or with ⌘I, the shelves collapse to their rail rather than hiding, so Classic's header needs no details toggle for them. The Simplified header keeps its toggle, which opens the shelves or collapses them.
- **Classic's controls move to its header.** Classic's header has no Stop, Wake or ⋯ of the agent's, so while this sidebar is chosen it gains one state button (Wake while paused, Interrupt mid-turn, else Pause) and ⋯ with Stop and splitting the tab, beside its Run gate and Delete. Its technical details are always drawn under Classic, which has no switch, in the footer and in Setup.
- **An agent expanded in place on the board has the same sidebar as its window**: the shelves, following the Agent sidebar setting (always, under Simplified), open or on the rail as they last were in any window or tile (⌘I). Its header holds the window's state button, as an icon, and ⋯ (Stop, splitting the tab), and under Simplified the shelves toggle as well. While the shelves are open its header leaves the branch, issue and pull requests to them, as the window's does; on the rail it says them again. The tile is a node of the board, so its body takes no board drag, scroll or arrow key: the sidebar's handle and tabs keep their own. Its width is the window's one choice, at least 200 px (tabs by icon below 240) and at most half the tile. A tile is never narrower than 640 px, so the least width always fits, but the default 316 px would leave a terminal under about 60 columns in one narrower than 800 px: such a tile opens its shelves on the rail, for itself alone, without changing the choice every other window and tile shares.
- **Sections fold, and stay as they were left.** Made's Shown to you and Hand-off, Crew's Agents it started and Subagents, and Setup's Where it works and Around it each fold to their heading, a chevron button that Enter or Space works. The choice is one for every window and tile, kept like the board menu's sections, and every section starts open. Folded, the heading says how many rows it holds, and wears a dot when they hold something that needs you (a crew agent or subagent that asks, a plan that waits for a decision) or a failed gate, so a fold never hides what is urgent in silence. The page's blocks do not fold: the agent writes them with no stable identity, and its first block says what it is doing. From the board and Made's open pull request, which carry what needs you, never fold either.
- **Shell windows and tiles, external sessions and any session window with no agent keep the details strip** under either choice.

## What Classic never sees

- **No Classic baseline moves for flag-on work.** Every change made for the Simplified design keeps `pnpm test:visual` passing with no updated baseline for the default UI. Its own screenshots are named with a `simplified-` prefix, and are added, never replaced.
- **Classic-only paths stay as they are.** A component that renders differently under the flag keeps its Classic branch unchanged; one that only restyles needs no branch at all.
- **The setting itself is the one Classic change**: one row at the end of Settings. The Agent sidebar setting is Classic's own, with Full, its old drawing, as its default.

## The technical layer

The Simplified design shows less by default: branches, models, pull requests, raw states, terminals and drawing tools sit behind one additive switch, Show technical details, which adds them back and never changes the layout. Every piece of flag-on work that shows something technical tags it as such, so that switch can add it, rather than deciding on its own whether to show it.

The creation toolbar is the exception for drawing tools and terminals: its Draw menu (a sticky note, text, a shape and a zone) shows with the switch off, as the chosen toolbar drawing has it, since it is the only button that makes a note or a zone. So is its terminal: the New agent button's menu adds a new terminal with the switch off too, as the chosen drawing has it, so a terminal is always a click away. Every tool's key works whatever the switch says.

An agent's shelves are the exception for pull requests: its Made shelf lists each pull request, with its checks, and its page's From the board names one whose checks fail or wait, with the switch off, as the chosen sidebar drawing has it, so an agent's open changes are never hidden. Its branch, folder, AI and model stay behind the switch, in its footer and its Setup shelf's Where it works.

- **The switch** is off by default and one per user. Main holds it in `technical.json`, beside `design.json` and for the same reason, and a new window's query carries it (`WINDOW_QUERY.technical`). It is in Settings › General › Design, under the Simplified design row, and in View › Show Technical Details, a checkbox with no key of its own (the obvious one, ⌥⌘D, is the system's Dock toggle). Main builds the menu bar again on every change to it or the design, so the item and the Settings switch follow each other and a pick in any window.
- **`data-technical` on the root** while the switch is on under the Simplified design (`renderer/src/technical.ts`). It is never set under Classic, whatever the switch says, so nothing Classic draws can tell it exists; Classic has no switch, and its View menu leaves the item out.
- **Tagging is one of two ways.** A technical element that only needs leaving out carries `data-technical-detail`, which `app.css` hides under the Simplified design while the root is unmarked. A component that renders something different, or a menu item that needs the layer and is disabled while it is off, reads `useTechnicalDetails()`, which is true under Classic and under the Simplified design only with the switch on (`shared/technical.ts`). Nothing reads the setting itself to decide what to show.
- **Showing it moves nothing untagged.** A hidden bit is out of the flow, so a tagged bit sits where its coming back moves nothing else: the end of a row, a line or a slot of its own. Where it can't, the component reserves the room itself and reads `useTechnicalDetails()` instead. The rule's `display: none !important` is meant to beat a component's own `display`, inline or not.

## Consequences

- Classic and the Simplified design share every component, so a fix to one reaches both, and nothing is built twice.
- Each component that renders differently carries a branch until Classic is retired or the Simplified design is dropped; `useSimplified()` finds them all.
- What the app measures in script follows the design as well as the theme: open terminals restyle and refit, a zone's tab measures its chips again and the colour rows are kept per theme and design. A text item's box is still measured when it is edited, so one made under the other design keeps its box until then.
- Its system fonts (SF Pro, SF Mono) aren't bundled, so its screenshots depend on the machine's fonts more than Classic's, whose fonts ship with the app.
